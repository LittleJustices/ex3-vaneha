Family is everything in Vaneha. Each of the [[gods|Ninety-Nine Stars]] (the true number has shifted historically, but the name is still used regardless) is god of a region of Vaneha, and spiritual parent to all who call that region their home. A clan therefore consists of many mortal families, who are made kin not by shared blood but shared patronage and a shared home. Adoption is common, both within and across clans, and an outsider who takes up residence in a clan's lands is considered as much a member of the clan as if they had been born there, at least in theory. Proud Vanehans say this makes them more openhearted, compassionate, and loyal than other peoples.

Each clan is led by one family, which has the clan god's favor. If the god is their clan's spiritual parent, the ruling family is the eldest sibling. They administer the clan's holdings and name local deputies to steward particular towns, estates, and tracts of land. These stewards[^1] in turn lease the land to tenant farmers or otherwise put it to use for the clan's and state's benefit—so long as they can pay their own lease to the ruling family, the steward is free to reap the profit.

[^1]: Across the Eight Directions outright calls them jitō, which has a weird ring to me since that's a relatively deep cut/specific terminology. Maybe it's fine.

As the ruling families are their clan's elders, so is the Sword Prince's line senior to the clans. The Sword Princes personally claim only the land immediately surrounding Ama-Ni-Traya, including the capital, as well as a few other strategic spots, but reserve for themselves the right to parcel out regions among the clans in exchange for taxes and corvée labor, legitimized by their possession of the Imperial Daiklave. Their patron likewise is foremost among the gods, the First and Hundredth of the Ninety-Nine Stars.

# Individual Clans
These clans have appeared in-game, directly or in passing.

## Anori

**Deity:** Chohie Ring-the-Peak

The Anori clan has been known for generations to produce Vaneha's finest geomancers, a highly respected vocation in this land. As such, they're staunch allies of the Sword, fabulously wealthy, and arguably the literal architects of the current order. It is the Anori who maintain Jibei's layout and monitor the flows of essence from Ama-Ni-Traya, and they who develop building codes and requirements. This prominent position also situates them as tastemakers and patrons of the arts, though outside of architecture they're content to elevate rather than lead the way.

The Anori's patron is Chohie, whose domain is the echoing mountain slopes and the roar of wind between peaks. Their great form, resembling a wrestler shaped from weathered rocks, is a fixture at Gensuji's court and their joyous, nimble dances are a highlight of any function and a popular motif for prints and paintings.
## Datei

**Deity:** Nazubi Gentle Shroud

Once, the Datei stood at the top. Their fall to the bottom became the stuff of plays, epics, and cautionary tales for generations since. Now they are a shadow-clan, without a homeland, which in Vaneha means they are nothing. Yet they have survived in exile by making themselves useful as shadows to the clans and to the Sword, and with the end of the civil war they clawed themselves a way back into the capital.

See [[Datei Assets]].
## Iachi

**Deity:** Jieke Blooms-in-Winter

Clan Iachi was once brought low by the Datei and has never forgotten this. The humiliation was repaid with interest a generation later, as any child knows, but the grudge remained. It made the clan patient and conservative, and ferocious in a crisis. Perhaps this is the influence of Jieke, god of winter crops and flowers. Under her auspices, Iachi flourished during the civil war, and while they weren't on its winning side, they were able to preserve their fields and emerge wealthy and strong.

Iachi rules the rolling hills of Orm, where the clan produces wines renowned throughout the East. A bottle of their finest vintages could pay for a Datei assassin, if either clan were willing to make that deal.
## Kakani

**Deity:** Kanagi the Certain

At one point Raizo's home clan. Notoriously self-involved, the crab god Kanagi largely lacks interest in the greater game of clan politics and is content to lead a minor clan charged with a minor domain, leaving Kakani's leadership to meet the bare minimum of their obligations to the sword as they see fit. But the inhabitants of Kanagi's domain know their god as a researcher and a scholar, not simply a surly hermit.
## Maon

**Deities:** Kamo and Moka of the Stony Banks

Clan Maon claims descent from a river people who have lived on the Yellow River since the days of the Shogunate, and though they have gained and lost territory over time, Maon has held on to one part or another of Vaneha's northern shore for as long as there has been a Vaneha. They wear their heritage proudly, dressing in their ancestral style even at court and on campaign in defiance of popular fashion—and they get away with it, for their fleet is the backbone of Vaneha's river trade and river warfare both. Clan Maon has two tutelary deities, the twin gods Kamo and Moka. Originally god-blooded daughters of a long-forgotten river deity and a solar hero of the First Age, they attained status as gods of river fishing through their father's machinations, then centuries later descended with the Ninety-Nine Stars to their old homeland.

Most recently, Clan Maon has reacquired a territory it lost generations ago in Nathir, in no small part thanks to the Circle's efforts. This puts them simultaneously in charge of a lucrative but damaged and resentful port, and in Clan Datei's debt.
## Otaya

**Deity:** Zao Sweet and Bright

The youngest clan, Otaya was founded at the end of the last period of civil war. Its patron god Zao has a history of association with the Guild, and the clan's primary mandate is to let Vaneha profit from their connections while keeping the Guild itself at arm's length as intermediaries.

Unusually among the Stars, newcomer Zao has a god-blooded son, Yano, who works closely with the clan's head but does not himself hold a leadership position.
## Saoto

**Deity:** Menai the Admirer

Though kept at arm's length for their anti-war stance and support of a losing side in the last civil war, the Saoto control several breadbasket provinces in Vaneha's southern foothills. These yield rich harvests, but demand much labor for maximum productivity. They oppose Gensuji's expansionist policies because the draft directly cuts into their labor force. In part, this resistance is ideological as well: the clan is traditionally led by a circle of elders, who are old enough to remember the time of civil war and have no desire to to take up arms again or see their grandchildren do it.

The Saoto deity did not descend from Heaven with the other Stars but was originally a terrestrial god of the Court of Earthly Spirits who joined the Ninety-Nine in their conquest as a turncoat. Whatever she was once the god of, it burned in that war, and the valley that held it now forms the core of the Saoto provinces.
## Uema

**Deity:** Megemi Ironhilt

Clan Uema rules in the city of Noru, where anvils ring day and night. It was the clan's patron, Megemi Ironhilt, who pioneered the forging of the sacred metal of Agate Lake, and to this day, the Uema arm Vaneha's soldiers, securing them a comfortable place among the clans closest to the Sword.
## The Imperial Clan

**Deity:** The First and Hundredth

The Sword Princes rule Vaneha, legitimized by the patronage of the First and Hundredth of the Ninety-Nine Stars and their possession of the Imperial Daiklave. They claim no provinces of their own save the capital Jibei and select strategic areas across the country but collect tribute from the clans and ensure their loyalty by means of the hostage-guests the Sword permanently entertains in Jibei. Possession of the Daiklave is hereditary, but the exact line of succession is determined within the imperial family by means outsiders are not privy to, likely involving the candidate's ability to wield the Imperial Daiklave.
