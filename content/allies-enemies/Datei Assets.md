# Datei Ma Kuromi

Ally 1: Kagemori's elder sister and representative (hostage) in the capital. She was named such as the ruling family's most distinguished child and the one expected to rule in the future and has made the role her life's work. The declaration that her younger brother would rule the clan instead came as a surprise to many, but apparently not her. Then again, to hide veils behind veils is the Datei way.

# Nazubi Gentle Shroud

Ally 3: the clan deity. Daimyo of summer rains and witness to secret contracts; patron of spies, affairs, and conspiracies. He covers his face with a thin, damp shroud at all times and to swear him to secrecy is to reveal yourself to him—but he is a trustworthy keeper of secrets.

When Clan Datei was great, his domain was the sweet summer rain which cools the air and speeds travel, the sunshower which blesses unions, the light fog which lends privacy to meetings. In their exile, his rains hide the sound of footsteps and the watchful eyes of his kin.

# Possessions

## The Datei Compound

The Datei residence is humble only in comparison to other clans' mansions. It takes up a block of its own, surrounded by a garden and receiving its own water via aqueduct, as demanded by law. Many clan hostages adorn their gardens in manners reminiscent of their home provinces, and so Kuromi, representing the family without a home, has had strange plants and rocks brought in from Dead Laris. Here is a boulder that has partially turned to glass at the center of an otherwise normal rock garden; there a shrub that's naturally grown to uncannily resemble a sleeping figure.

The garden's central pond always reflects a lightly clouded sky, regardless of the actual weather. This is the entrance to Nazubi's sanctum. Even clan members are rarely invited in, as he prefers to entertain visitors in the garden over tea.
## Datei War-Mask

"Gifted" to Kagemori by Clan Anori as a token of appreciation for the Circle's discreet rescue of the Anori hostages. It depicts a warrior in their final moments, snarling at death. The mask is old but exquisitely wrought and hardly worn. Its color reminds of yellowed ivory, but it's cool to the touch, as if it were in fact made of stone. The warrior's pointed eyebrows and mustache, swept fiercely outward, are made of many individual threads of shiny grey metal inlaid into the mask. They glitter like a prism as they catch the light and resonate with something at the touch.

Though Kagemori doesn't know it yet, it gives its wearer two abilities:
- When a warrior under their command or otherwise fighting for their sake dies, the wearer can learn the deceased's most dearly held defining intimacy as if through an automatically successful reflexive read intentions action, if the dead warrior was significant enough in the story to have their intimacies represented individually.
- The wearer can make a special (dicepool to be determined) roll to introduce facts involving the defining or major intimacies of warriors who have died fighting for them or under their command.

# Dead Laris

When foreigners hear of Dead Laris, they often imagine a vast Shadowland, a black scar upon Creation. But the fate of the Pox Plains is both more and less tragic than this, for the silence that hangs over its dry lands are all too much of this world. Once, a civilization thrived on the banks of Hundred Tongues Lake, nestled between the Smoky, Maruto, and Sandy rivers. A rich if politically unimportant province of the Shogunate, the lands of Laris emerged from the dual catastrophes of the Great Contagion and the Raksha invasion less damaged than many other Eastern provinces, due in part due to drawing back to the archipelago in the center of the lake. Aided by a population of First Age Automatons, Laris began to rebuild, no longer a mere province but a powerful federation of city-states. Unity would not last, however, and struggles to lead the federation grew more bitter and more intense with each passing generation, until mortal greed did what even Balor could not, with the vaporization of the entire lake in the event referred to as Hesiesh's Breath. Stories often claim this was due to some singular weapon, but this is likely just to simplify the stories. Where lush waters once were are now dusty plains, and where great cities once stood are now ruins.
